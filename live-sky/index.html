RA and declination are apparent geocentric coordinates of date. Local altitude and azimuth use your position on Earth's surface, including lunar parallax. AU = the mean Earth–Sun distance unit.

How accurate is this view?

Positions are calculated at the displayed instant with Astronomy Engine 2.1.19, whose target angular accuracy is about one arcminute (1/60°). The engine is self-hosted: no live data service or API key is required. Live mode follows your device clock and refreshes positions once per second while this page is visible.

The space view uses apparent Earth-centered vectors with light-travel-time and aberration corrections. It includes the real ecliptic latitude of each body. Rings are distance guides in the ecliptic plane, not orbits around Earth. Planet sizes, surfaces, and ring orientation are illustrative; use the numerical phase and distance readouts for physical values. Compressed mode preserves 3D directions while squeezing the radial distances. True-distance mode preserves distance ratios, so the Moon and inner bodies cluster close to Earth.

The local sky looks upward with north at the top and east at the left. Only bodies above the horizon are drawn; daylight bodies may be impossible to see. No background star catalog is plotted. Rise/set estimates search the next 48 hours using a level horizon and standard refraction. Terrain and weather can shift actual times; polar locations may have no event in that window.

Constellations use IAU boundaries, not astrological zodiac signs. The ecliptic coordinates in the details and CSV use the true equinox of date. For the existing Lahiri sidereal charts, use Planetary Transits. Supported dates: 1900–2099. This is an educational viewer, not a navigation instrument.
